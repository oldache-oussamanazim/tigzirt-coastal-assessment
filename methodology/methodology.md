# Methodology

## 1. Field-study approach
The field study covered Tasssalest and Feraoun beaches along the Tigzirt coast. It combined topographic measurements, GPS positioning, direct field observations, inspection of historical imagery, and brief discussions with local residents.

## 2. Topographic survey
**Equipment:** Topcon GM-52 total station, reflector prism, and Garmin eTrex GPS.

As described in the report, the total station was installed at a stable location with a clear view of the beach. A survey job was created to record measurements. The reflector prism was positioned successively at selected points along the shoreline. Distances and angles were measured to obtain point coordinates (X, Y, Z), which were stored in the instrument for later processing.

The Garmin eTrex GPS was used to locate survey stations and fixed reference points. **AutoCAD was used**.

## 3. Historical shoreline comparison using Google Earth
A historical comparison was conducted for Tassalest using Google Earth imagery. A fixed point on the backshore protection wall was used as a reference **(reported coordinates: 36°53'23"N, 4°06'32"E)**.

The decreasing distance between the fixed point and the waterline show's that the shoreline has retreated.

### Scope and limitations
This is a reference-distance comparison, not a complete extraction of shoreline polylines, a beach-wide transect analysis, or a DSAS-based assessment. The report does not document positional uncertainty or tidal/water-level normalization. The 6.40 m difference is therefore described as a local change in the measured reference distance, not as a general erosion rate for the beach.

## 4. Geomorphological and environmental observations
The report describes beach sediment types (fine-to-medium sands, gravels and pebbles, and local marly/clayey materials), sediment redistribution by waves and coastal currents, stream channels and mouth deposits, localized slope instability, and coastal infrastructure such as gabions, a backshore protection wall, culverts, and port breakwaters.

These are observations and interpretations recorded in the field report. 

## 5. Local knowledge
Brief exchanges with residents were used to collect qualitative observations about coastal change, landslides, and flooding. These are treated as contextual information, not instrument-based measurements.

## 6. Reproducibility and future additions
To reproduce or extend the topographic component, the following materials are needed:
- Original Topcon GM-52 survey export or coordinate table.
- AutoCAD drawing file(s).
- Available field notes describing instrument setup, control points, and survey date.
- Original Google Earth image dates and saved shoreline/reference measurements.

These materials are not currently included. They may be added after recovery and verification. Missing coordinates, dates, and processing steps have not been reconstructed or invented.
