# Methodology

## 1. Field-study approach
The field study covered Tasalast and Feraoun beaches along the Tigzirt coast. It combined topographic measurements, GPS positioning, direct field observations, inspection of historical imagery, and brief discussions with local residents.

## 2. Topographic survey
**Equipment:** Topcon GM-52 total station, reflector prism, and Garmin eTrex GPS.

As described in the report, the total station was installed at a stable location with a clear view of the beach. A survey job was created to record measurements. The reflector prism was positioned successively at selected points along the shoreline. Distances and angles were measured to obtain point coordinates (X, Y, Z), which were stored in the instrument for later processing.

The Garmin eTrex GPS was used to locate survey stations and fixed reference points. The author confirms that **AutoCAD was used**. The report mentions AutoCAD and Covadis as examples of processing software, but the original CAD files and coordinate exports are not currently available. No additional processing workflow or derived topographic result is asserted here.

## 3. Historical shoreline comparison using Google Earth
A historical comparison was conducted for Tasalast using Google Earth imagery. A fixed point on the backshore protection wall was used as a reference (reported coordinates: 36°53'23"N, 4°06'32"E).

The report text records:
- November 2019: 24.04 m
- 2020: 20.15 m
- November 2023: 17.64 m

The report interprets the decreasing distance between the fixed point and the waterline as local shoreline retreat.

### Date discrepancy
The subsection heading states **November 2019 – October 2021 – November 2023**, while the paragraph states **November 2019 – 2020 – November 2023**. The middle date should be verified against the original Google Earth imagery.

### Scope and limitations
This is a reference-distance comparison, not a complete extraction of shoreline polylines, a beach-wide transect analysis, or a DSAS-based assessment. The report does not document positional uncertainty, image georeferencing uncertainty, or tidal/water-level normalization. The 6.40 m difference is therefore described as a local change in the measured reference distance, not as a general erosion rate for the beach.

## 4. Geomorphological and environmental observations
The report describes beach sediment types (fine-to-medium sands, gravels and pebbles, and local marly/clayey materials), sediment redistribution by waves and coastal currents, stream channels and mouth deposits, localized slope instability, and coastal infrastructure such as gabions, a backshore protection wall, culverts, and port breakwaters.

These are observations and interpretations recorded in the field report. This repository does not independently validate the causal explanations or quantify sediment transport and slope movement.

## 5. Local knowledge
Brief exchanges with residents were used to collect qualitative observations about coastal change, landslides, and flooding. These are treated as contextual information, not instrument-based measurements.

## 6. Reproducibility and future additions
To reproduce or extend the topographic component, the following materials are needed:
- Original Topcon GM-52 survey export or coordinate table.
- AutoCAD drawing file(s).
- Available field notes describing instrument setup, control points, and survey date.
- Original Google Earth image dates and saved shoreline/reference measurements.

These materials are not currently included. They may be added after recovery and verification. Missing coordinates, dates, and processing steps have not been reconstructed or invented.
